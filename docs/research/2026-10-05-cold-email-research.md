# Cold Emailing Recruiters for Internships (SWE and Design): Research Report
*Generated: 2026-10-05 | Sources: ~70 consulted, ~55 cited | Confidence: Medium*

Almost all reply-rate figures come from B2B sales datasets published by vendors that sell outreach tools (Hunter, Belkins, Lavender, Woodpecker, Gong/30MPC). Use them to decide which way to lean, not as exact numbers. The guidance specific to internships comes from first-hand recruiter and hiring-manager accounts.

## Executive summary
1. **Keep it short.** 60–120 words, 3–5 sentences, plain text, readable on a phone. Sources disagree on the exact best length. Hunter (34M emails) finds that relevance matters more than word count. The direction everyone agrees on is "short".
2. **One specific hook, one proof, one ask.** The hook should be something only this team would recognise. The proof is one linked project with a concrete result. The ask is a single clear request.
3. **At companies with a formal internship program, apply first, then email.** Name the role and requisition. Wait a few days after applying; don't email on the day you apply.
4. **The subject line names the role.** Use roughly 2–7 words or under 50 characters, e.g. `Applied: SWE Intern, Summer 2027 – <Name>`. Avoid "Internship Application" and "Quick question".
5. **The follow-up is the biggest lever.** Send 1 follow-up (at most 2) in the same thread, about 5–7 days later, with something new. After that, contact a *different* person, one at a time.
6. **Design differs mainly in the work sample.** Deep-link the one relevant case study. If it has a password, put the password next to the link, because a missing password is an "automatic pass". Don't put images or GIFs in the first email.

## 1. Length and structure
- 50–125 words averaged 8.2% replies, versus 3.9% at 200–300 words ([Overloop aggregate](https://overloop.com/blog/whats-the-best-email-length-for-sales-outreach)). 75–100 words peaked in a 3M-email set ([Sales.co](https://howlongshouldacoldemailbe.com/blog/optimal-cold-email-length-data/)).
- Hunter found 20–39 words best, but every length band fell between 3.4% and 4.5%, so word count barely matters next to relevance ([Hunter](https://hunter.io/blog/cold-email-word-count/)).
- Student data: 11.9% replies under 100 words versus 1.9% at 100–200 ([Whali, 6,337 student emails](https://whali.com/blog/cold-email-data-study-students-2026)). Single source; the under-100 group was only 59 emails.
- Skeleton: specific opener → one line of credentials → one proof with a link → one ask → sign-off with links ([Nick Singh](https://www.nicksingh.com/posts/cold-email-tips-to-land-your-dream-job-with-examples), [Codédex](https://www.codedex.io/blog/gabbys-guide-to-cold-emailing-when-we-werent-even-hiring), [TK Kong, interns.design](https://interns.substack.com/p/how-to-land-a-design-job-interview)).
- Format for mobile. The first read is 8x more likely to happen on a phone ([Lavender](https://lavender.ai/blog/5-takeaways-from-our-latest-cold-email-benchmark-report)).

## 2. Subject lines
- 2–4 words got the most opens (46%); beyond 10 words opens fell to 34%. Personalised subjects lifted replies from 3% to 7% ([Belkins, 5.5M](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics)).
- For a note sent after applying, name the role and your name ([techloop](https://www.techloop.news/p/how-to-cold-email-recruiters-new), [DearHiringManager](https://dearhiringmanager.io/blog/email-hiring-manager-after-applying)).
- Avoid "Following up on my application" (Workie's current subject), "Internship Application" and "Quick question" ([JobHuntrr](https://www.jobhuntrr.com/blog/cold-email-for-internship), [FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/cold-emails-for-internships/)).

## 3. Personalisation
- It is the strongest signal in the data: replies went from 3% to 7% (Belkins) and from 3.6% to 5.6% with two custom details ([Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)).
- Mention their product or engineering work: a blog post, a shipped flow, a design trade-off. Not the alma mater or "I love your mission" ([30MPC/Gong](https://tactics.30mpc.com/hubfs/The%20Ultimate%20Cold%20Email%20Data%20Report-1.pdf), [Hopin](https://hopinjobs.com/blog/cold-email-for-internship-india)).
- Text that reads as AI-written gets ignored ([Codédex](https://www.codedex.io/blog/gabbys-guide-to-cold-emailing-when-we-werent-even-hiring)). Generated drafts must stay plain and specific.

## 4. Call to action
- **To recruiters, be direct:** "I'd appreciate being considered for <role>; if you're not the right recruiter, could you point me to who is?" ([Nick Singh](https://www.nicksingh.com/posts/cold-email-tips-to-land-your-dream-job-with-examples), [Legagneux, campus recruiter](https://medium.com/@mathieu.legagneux/how-to-reach-out-to-recruiters-to-land-your-dream-internship-or-first-full-time-job-c7c3e0bd6685)).
- **To engineers, design managers and founders, keep the ask small:** 15 minutes, with an easy way out ([ADPList](https://adplist.org/guides/finding-a-mentor)).
- Never ask a stranger for a referral in the first email. Don't ask a recruiter to skip the process or review your CV (Legagneux).

## 5. Timing and follow-ups
- Timing is a weak effect. Send Tuesday to Thursday, in the morning or around midday in the recipient's time zone ([Belkins, 7.5M](https://belkins.io/blog/best-time-to-send-email): 8am–noon gave the most replies). Whali contradicts Belkins on Thursday, so don't over-optimise.
- After applying, wait 3–7 days ([DearHiringManager](https://dearhiringmanager.io/blog/email-hiring-manager-after-applying), [SentinelOne recruiter](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/messaging-recruiter-after-applying-job-follow-6-steps-rabczenko-xpr1f), [techloop](https://www.techloop.news/p/how-to-cold-email-recruiters-new)).
- One follow-up raised replies from 9% to 13% ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/follow-up-statistics/)). Follow-ups bring in 58.6% of all replies ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)). Job-search sources say 1, at most 2; repeated nudges read as desperate ([Pearable](https://pearable.me/knowledgebase/email-bypass-hr-hiring-manager)).

## 6. Who to target
- **Large companies:** the university or campus recruiter. **Mid-size:** the technical recruiter. **Startups under ~50 people:** the founder, CTO or design lead, who reply at roughly 2x the rate ([Nick Singh](https://www.nicksingh.com/posts/cold-email-tips-to-land-your-dream-job-with-examples), [Whali](https://whali.com/blog/sent-100-cold-emails-internship-results)).
- Use engineers and designers for chats. Use recruiters for the pipeline itself.

## 7. Software engineering specifics
- Link one project with a number (users, requests per second, latency): a deployed URL or a public repo. "If the link needs a login, it does not exist" ([Hopin](https://hopinjobs.com/blog/cold-email-for-internship-india)).
- Recruiters prefer the résumé attached as a PDF (Nick Singh, Legagneux). Workie's sender has no attachments yet, so for now it links LinkedIn and GitHub instead. The recruiter already has the résumé from the application.

## 8. Design specifics
- Reviewers spend 30 seconds to 2 minutes per portfolio ([Sophia Liu, Meta](http://www.studiosophy.com/blog/2022/4/13/how-i-got-my-faang-design-job), [Halim/ADPList panel](https://medium.com/@jameshalim16/an-in-depth-journey-on-how-i-landed-my-first-design-internship-2cc71176c387)).
- Deep-link one relevant case study and say in one sentence why it's relevant. A bare homepage link makes the reader do the work ([Simpson](https://uxdesign.cc/tips-for-landing-your-next-product-design-job-98934af22526), [Trackr](https://trackr.help/en/blog/portfolio-vs-cv-developers-designers)).
- **Passwords** (seven or more independent reviewers agree): put the password next to the link, or don't use one. "If I can't open it in 5 seconds, I move on" ([Beserra](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/phelipes_productdesign-hiring-uxui-activity-7421530510616727554-dFY8), [Andrews](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/christopherjonandrews_psa-if-youre-applying-to-a-design-role-activity-7303146806509518848-TxKh), [Hope](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jasminehope_this-is-the-bane-of-my-existence-today-ive-activity-7188668722427977732-6bkv)).
- State your exact availability dates ([WebPeak](https://webpeak.org/blog/graphic-design-intership)).
- No images or GIFs in the first email. Hunter found images made no difference to replies ([Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)). Save visuals for a follow-up.
- The email itself is read as a sample of your communication design ([Adithi N](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/adithi-n-759979232_this-cold-email-got-me-10-design-interviews-activity-7432785033762889728-4uNm)).

## 9. Finding recruiter emails
- **The public ATS APIs never return the recruiter.** Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby expose no recruiter or hiring-manager fields ([Greenhouse](https://developers.greenhouse.io/job-board.html), [Lever](https://github.com/lever/postings-api), [Ashby](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api)). The only free source is an address written in the posting text.
- **Hunter:** 50 free credits a month. It offers Domain Search (`department=hr`, `type=personal|generic`, which returns `accept_all`, `pattern` and per-email `verification`), Email Finder and Email Verifier. It returns HTTP 451 for people who have opted out; respect it ([Hunter API](https://hunter.io/api-documentation/v2)).
- **Findymail:** API on every plan, and it charges only for verified emails. `POST /api/search/domain {domain, roles}` finds a role at a company ([docs](https://app.findymail.com/docs/)). Prospeo has a `only_verified_email` mode. Clearbit's free API is gone.
- **Catch-all domains** accept every address, so no SMTP check can prove a guessed mailbox exists. Outbound port 25 is blocked on most networks. **Never send to an unverified guess.**
- **Off-limits sources:** GitHub's Acceptable Use Policy §7 forbids using commit emails for unsolicited email. LinkedIn's terms ban scraping (*hiQ v. LinkedIn*, 2022).
- **CAN-SPAM** covers commercial email. A one-to-one job note is very likely outside it, but still: truthful From and subject, and stop when asked.

## Templates
**SWE, sent after applying, to a recruiter (~95 words).** Subject: `Applied: <Role> – <Name>`
> Hi <First>, I applied to the <Role> role at <Company> on <date>. I'm a <year> CS student at <School>. Most relevant: I built <project>, <one line> (<link>), and last summer at <org> I <result with a number>. <One line on why this team>. I'd appreciate being considered. If you're not the right recruiter, could you point me to whoever is? Thanks, <Name> · <GitHub> · <LinkedIn>

**Design, sent after applying, to a recruiter (~90 words).** Subject: `Applied: <Role> – <Name>`
> Hi <First>, I applied for the <Role> role on <date>. I'm a <year> studying <major> at <school>, available <dates>. The project most relevant to <Company> is <case study>: <problem → what I did → result>. Case study: <deep link> (password: <pw>). Happy to answer anything. Thanks for reviewing applications! <Name> · <portfolio> · <LinkedIn>

## Doubtful or single-source claims
- FirstSales cites a "NickSingh.com 2,000-email study". That study doesn't appear in Nick Singh's article.
- Whali: Thursday is the worst day.
- Lavender: individual contributors reply most.
- InternQuest: alumni reply 20–40% of the time.
- Asapu: leaving the portfolio link out of the first message raises replies.
- Majidova: cold outreach to recruiters doesn't work at all. This is the main counter-anecdote.

## Methodology
Three parallel research passes (SWE, design, email discovery) using Firecrawl and Exa search, with 13 sources read in full. The Firecrawl account reported low credits.
